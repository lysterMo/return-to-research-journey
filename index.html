Return to Research:
Evidence-informed researcher journey map

The central finding: returning to work is an administrative and employment transition; returning to research is a longer process of rebuilding continuity, relationships, responsibilities and career momentum.

This map draws on White Rose research and emerging findings. It is a cross-institutional, composite journey, not the experience of one individual or a verified map of processes.

Cross-cutting conditions shaping the journey

These are not additional stages. They influence what happens throughout.

◈
Research career structure

Grant timelines, fixed-term contracts, publication cycles, authorship and promotion expectations continue to operate during leave.

▦
Institutional arrangements

HR processes, workload models, School decisions and specialist support may operate through different systems.

♡
Individual circumstances

Recovery, caring responsibilities, financial circumstances and personal preferences shape what support is useful.

◎
Navigational capital

Researchers have unequal access to institutional knowledge, professional networks, financial flexibility and confidence in approaching decision-makers.

The analytical framing describes the interaction between research career systems, institutional systems and human life transitions. It also identifies navigational capital as an important source of unequal experience. Sources: White Rose emerging findings; Researcher Development presentation.

Where the journey is most vulnerable

Interpretive synthesis — not a measured risk score

The evidence draws particular attention to planning decisions, preparation for return and the months of research rebuilding. These are priorities for further investigation, not statistically ranked stages. The bar heights are illustrative only.

How researchers can use this map

In a live design lab and asynchronous activity, present the journey as a draft to challenge, asking researchers:

  1. Which parts reflect your understanding, and which do not?
  2. What important experiences, circumstances or positive practices are missing?
  3. Where should support be available without someone having to know whom to ask?
  4. What would make the greatest practical difference?
  5. What might an apparently helpful intervention get wrong?

Then use contextual inquiry with School and institutional colleagues to investigate how those touchpoints actually operate.

Important evidence boundaries

This map is grounded in the White Rose synthesis and supporting material. It does not establish that every researcher experiences every stage or difficulty. It also does not claim that the seven stages are equally distinct in practice.

No emotional curve or numerical severity scores have been inferred: the available material supports variation in experiences, not a single emotional trajectory.

Local processes remain to be mapped and verified. The October discussion provides useful initial local evidence, including research mentoring and flexible arrangements, but does not establish an end-to-end service.

Source: Supporting Return to Research — University of York, ACT School meeting notes (13 October 2026).
